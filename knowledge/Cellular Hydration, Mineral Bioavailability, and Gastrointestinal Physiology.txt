2. The Mathematics of Daily Water Loss
The body possesses no internal reservoir or "reserve tank" for water; it relies entirely on daily intake to offset continuous physiological output. In an average 24-hour period, a human loses approximately 2.5 liters (or 2.5 quarts) of water through basic metabolic functions:
* Kidneys (Urination): 1.5 liters
* Skin (Perspiration): 0.5 liters (Increases dramatically with exercise or saunas)
* Colon (Feces): 0.3 liters
* Lungs (Respiration): 0.2 liters
Rule of Thumb for Hydration: The body requires 1 quart (or 1 liter) of pure water per 50 lbs of body weight daily. For example, a 100 lb individual requires 2 quarts; a 200 lb individual requires up to 4 quarts (though this must be scaled gradually). 
* Note on Dehydrating Agents: Coffee, non-herbal tea (tannins), alcohol, and refined sugar act as aggressive diuretics. Pathological studies indicate it requires 5 glasses of pure water just to replace the hydration lost from a single cup of sweetened coffee.
